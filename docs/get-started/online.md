# Run your first search online

Turn a question into a reading list on [paperseek.app](https://paperseek.app/) — no installation. About five minutes.

Prefer your own machine? [Install the Community edition](local.md).

## Before you start

- An email address to create an account. During the public beta every account receives **20 free credits a day**; a search costs **1 credit**. See [Service & credits](../online/credits.md).
- No account? You can run one temporary search without signing in — see [Search without signing in](../online/privacy.md#search-without-signing-in). Temporary searches are not saved.

## 1. Sign in

Open [paperseek.app](https://paperseek.app/) and click **Not signed in** at the bottom of the sidebar, then choose **Sign in or create an account**. Enter your email address, confirm the verification code, and set a password. Details and other sign-in methods: [Your account](../online/account.md).

## 2. Ask a question

After sign-in, **PaperSeek Service** is the active mode: models are provided by PaperSeek and tasks use your daily credits.

Open **Search** in the sidebar. Keep the default source **OpenAlex**, leave the budget on **Auto**, and type your question, for example:

> How does open-source software obtain funding for long-term maintenance?

Select **Start search**, or press **Ctrl+Enter / ⌘+Enter**. The form shows the credits the task uses and how many remain today.

## 3. Follow the search

PaperSeek works in four visible stages: it interprets your question, iterates source-specific queries, retrieves and ranks candidates, then presents results. You can stop the run at any time from the header. What the stages and counts mean: [Search the literature](../guide/search.md).

## 4. Read the results

Open **Results** when the run finishes. Each paper shows its abstract, a relevance score with a written reason, and a link to the original record. Export the list as CSV, JSON, BibTeX, or RIS.

The run is saved to your account. Reopen it from **Search history** in the sidebar. From here you can also open a [citation map](../guide/citation-map.md), run a [bibliometric analysis](../guide/bibliometrics.md), or start an [experimental review](../guide/reviews.md).
